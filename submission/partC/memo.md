# Part C — Decision Memo: Casual Indic Tone

## Recommendation: Path (a) — SFT on synthetic "casualized" pairs

### Assumptions

- FLM-4B-Instruct can already generate grammatically correct Indic text; the
  issue is register/formality, not capability.
- "Casual" can be defined operationally as: contractions, colloquial vocabulary,
  shorter sentences, informal address (तू/तुम vs. आप in Hindi).
- Synthetic casual pairs can be generated by prompting the model itself to
  rewrite its own formal outputs — quality is sufficient for SFT with reviewer
  filtering.
- Languages without a reviewer (Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi) can
  transfer some casual patterns from Hindi SFT, though quality will be lower.

### Back-of-envelope arithmetic

**Data generation** (A100-80GB, ~2 days):
- 6 languages × 5,000 formal→casual response pairs = 30,000 pairs
- At ~500 tok/pair × 30K = 15M tokens. Generation at ~200 tok/s → ~21 hours

**Human review** (10 h/week × 2 weeks × 2 languages):
- Reviewer covers Hindi + Kannada at ~50 pairs/hour
- 10 h/week × 2 weeks = 20 h → 1,000 reviewed pairs per language
- Remaining 4,000 per language: auto-filtered by length/perplexity heuristics

**SFT training** (~4 hours on A100-80GB):
- 30K pairs × 500 tok = 15M tokens. At 4B params, fp16, 1 epoch ≈ 4 hours
- Total A100 time: ~2.5 days generation + 0.2 days training = 2.7 days of 14

**Serving cost**: Zero marginal cost — same model, same inference stack.

### Success metric

**Human preference rate ≥ 60%** on a 200-pair blind eval set (casual SFT
output vs. baseline formal output), judged by the reviewer for Hindi and
Kannada. For unreviewed languages, automated metric: mean response length
decrease ≥ 15% and no regression on a 100-question correctness eval
(accuracy ≥ 95% of baseline).

### Kill criterion

**After week 1**: evaluate the first 500 reviewed Hindi pairs. If the reviewer
rates < 40% of SFT outputs as "appropriately casual and natural-sounding,"
abandon SFT and fall back to prompt engineering (path c) for the 3-week launch.
Rationale: if the synthetic data pipeline can't produce quality casual text even
with review, more data won't fix it — the model lacks the casual register in
its pretraining distribution.

### Day 1 experiment

1. Prompt the model to rewrite 100 formal Hindi responses into casual tone.
2. Have the reviewer rate each as: ✓ casual & correct, ✗ still formal, or
   ✗ casual but wrong.
3. If ≥ 70% are ✓, the synthetic pipeline works → proceed with full generation.
   If < 50%, consider prompt-engineering (path c) as a faster fallback.

This validates both the generation pipeline and the reviewer's calibration
before committing the A100 to 2 days of generation.
